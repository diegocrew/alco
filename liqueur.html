THE FIELD BOARD

From Monastery Medicine to the Back Bar

Origin Story

1300s • Monastic Elixirs

Herbs as Medicine

European monasteries macerated herbs, roots and barks in spirit as remedies. Bénédictine and Chartreuse both descend directly from these apothecary recipes.

1860s • Amaro Italiano

The Bitter Boom

Italian pharmacists commercialised bitter digestive tonics. Campari, Fernet-Branca and Aperol turned medicinal bitterness into a national drinking culture.

Modern • The Sugar Line

What Makes a Liqueur

EU law draws the line at sugar: a liqueur must carry at least 100 g of sugar per litre. Below that threshold you have a flavoured spirit, not a liqueur.
